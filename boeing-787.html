About the Boeing 787-10

The Boeing 787-10 is the largest member of the 787 Dreamliner family. It was created by stretching the 787-9 fuselage while retaining the same basic wing and advanced technologies.

The 787-10 made its first flight on 31 March 2017. It was developed for airlines looking for a high-capacity wide-body aircraft with strong efficiency on medium- and long-haul routes.

With a length of 68.3 metres, the 787-10 is longer than the 787-8 and 787-9, while retaining the family's distinctive 60.1-metre wingspan.

💡 Did You Know?

The 787's airframe contains approximately 50% composites by weight. These materials help reduce weight and improve resistance to corrosion compared with traditional aluminium structures.

⚙️ Dreamliner Engines

The Boeing 787-10 can be equipped with two different engine families. Airlines can select between General Electric's GEnx-1B and Rolls-Royce's Trent 1000.

General Electric GEnx-1B

A high-bypass turbofan developed specifically for modern wide-body aircraft. The GEnx family is designed to provide strong fuel efficiency and reduced emissions.

Rolls-Royce Trent 1000

A high-bypass turbofan from Rolls-Royce's Trent family, offering airlines an alternative engine option for the 787 Dreamliner.
